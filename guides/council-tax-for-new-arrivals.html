Council tax explained for new arrivals

Council tax is a monthly local tax on your home. It's one of the first bills to set up after you move in, and there's a common discount many people miss.

What it pays for

Council tax funds local services such as bin collections, roads, libraries and social care. It's charged per home, not per person, and is usually paid by the people who live there, including tenants.

How much it costs

Each home in England and Scotland is placed in a band (A to H in England) based on its value, and your council sets the charge for each band. In Wales there are bands A to I. The amount varies a lot between areas, so check your exact charge on your council's website before you agree a rent.

Discounts to claim

  • Living alone: a 25% single-person discount. If your partner hasn't joined you yet, you may qualify until they arrive.
  • Full-time students are usually disregarded, so a home where everyone is a student may pay nothing.
  • Your council may offer reductions if you're on a low income.

How to set it up

  1. Find your council using your postcode on GOV.UK.
  2. Register as the person responsible from the day you moved in.
  3. Choose to pay over 10 or 12 months. Twelve smaller payments are easier to budget for.
  4. Tell the council when anyone moves in or out, as it can change your bill.

Common questions

Do tenants pay council tax?

Usually, yes. Council tax is normally paid by the people who live in the home, including tenants, unless your tenancy says it's included in the rent.

How much is the single-person discount?

25% off your bill if you're the only adult living in the home.

Can I pay council tax monthly?

Yes. Councils usually offer 10 monthly payments, and many let you choose 12.
