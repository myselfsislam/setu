Moving to the UK from India: your first 90 days money checklist

Everything to sort out with your money in India and the UK, in the order most people need it.

Before you leave, or straight after: in India

  1. Change your savings account to NRO. Once you move abroad for work, your bank must be told. Your normal savings account becomes an NRO account (for money earned in India). The account number usually stays the same, so EMIs keep running.
  2. Open an NRE account for money you send from the UK. NRE interest is tax-free in India and the money can be moved back out freely.
  3. Update your mutual funds and shares. Tell each fund house and your broker that you now live abroad, so they update your status and linked bank account.
  4. Tell your lenders and card providers which account to take payments from, so nothing bounces.
  5. Keep your Indian phone number active. Bank OTPs, UPI and Aadhaar all depend on it.

Weeks 1–2 in the UK

  1. Set up your eVisa. Employers and landlords check your status online with a share code. GOV.UK: eVisas
  2. Open a UK bank account so you can be paid. Many app-based banks only need a passport and UK address.
  3. Get your National Insurance number. GOV.UK: apply for a National Insurance number
  4. Register with a GP. It's free, and your visa's health surcharge already covers NHS care.
  5. Set up council tax. If you live alone you can usually get 25% off. GOV.UK: council tax

Month 1

  1. Check your first payslip. For most people the tax code is 1257L. An emergency code such as 0T, W1 or M1 can mean you're paying too much for now. GOV.UK: tax codes
  2. Keep one month of rent and bills in your UK account before investing anything.
  3. Pick a low-cost way to send money home. Compare the rupees delivered, not just the fee.
  4. Keep proof of your arrival date (boarding pass or passport stamp). It affects your tax position in both countries.

By day 90

  1. Build an emergency fund of 3–6 months of UK costs.
  2. Look at your workplace pension. You're enrolled automatically, and your employer adds money too.
  3. Learn about ISAs: up to £20,000 a year of saving or investing with no UK tax. GOV.UK: ISAs
  4. Check UK tax on your Indian income. As a UK resident, your worldwide income can be taxed here. New arrivals may be able to claim relief on foreign income for 4 years through Self Assessment. GOV.UK: tax on foreign income
  5. Plan your Indian tax return. If you spent under 182 days in India in the year you left, you usually file as an NRI.

Common questions

Do I have to change my Indian savings account after moving to the UK?

Yes. Once you become a non-resident Indian, resident savings accounts should be changed to NRO accounts. Your bank can usually do this without changing the account number.

What is the difference between NRO and NRE accounts?

An NRO account holds money earned in India, such as rent or interest, and its interest is taxable in India. An NRE account holds money earned abroad, its interest is tax-free in India, and the money can be moved back out freely.

What tax code should be on my first UK payslip?

Most people with one job have the tax code 1257L. Codes such as 0T, W1 or M1 are emergency codes and can mean you are paying too much tax until HMRC updates them.
