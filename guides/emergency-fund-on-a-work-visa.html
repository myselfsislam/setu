How big should your emergency fund be on a work visa?

On a sponsored visa, losing your job starts a clock. A cash cushion gives you time to find a new sponsor without panic.

Why it matters more on a work visa

If your sponsored job ends, your employer must tell the Home Office, which usually shortens your permission to stay to around 60 days (or less if your visa ends sooner). In that time you need to find a new sponsor, switch visa or leave. Savings mean you can focus on the right job, not the first one.

How many months to save

  • Start with one month of rent and bills sitting in your current account.
  • Build to 3 months of essential UK costs as your first proper target.
  • Aim for 4 to 6 months if you're the only earner, have a family here, or pay large EMIs in India. Remember flights home and a deposit if you have to move.

What to count

Rent, council tax, energy, phone, food, travel, and any payments you must keep making in India, like loan EMIs. Leave out investing and nice-to-haves.

Where to keep it

An easy-access savings account in the UK, in pounds, so you can reach it quickly. A cash ISA keeps the interest free of UK tax. Avoid shares or locked deposits for this money.

Build it alongside Indian EMIs

  1. Pay off very expensive debt, like a credit card balance, first.
  2. Then split what's left each month between the emergency fund and other goals.
  3. Once the fund is full, move that money into long-term investing.

Common questions

How long do I have to find a new job on a Skilled Worker visa?

Usually about 60 days after the Home Office shortens your permission, or less if your visa expires sooner. Check your own documents and get immigration advice if it happens.

How many months should my emergency fund cover?

Build to at least 3 months of essential UK costs, and 4 to 6 months if you're the only earner, have family here, or pay large EMIs in India.

Where should I keep my emergency fund?

In an easy-access UK savings account or cash ISA, in pounds, so you can reach it quickly.
